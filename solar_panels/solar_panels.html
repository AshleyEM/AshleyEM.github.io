Solar Energy In Bangladesh

Bangladesh is a small country residing in South Asia, east of India. It has a population of about 163 million with more than half of its citizens living in rural regions (World Bank, 2019a; World Bank, 2019c). While electric power consumption in Bangladesh has risen exponentially in the past decade, more than one-fourth of the rural population remains without electricity (World Bank, 2019b; Hutt, 2020).

Solar Energy

Solar energy is emerging as a common source of power for homes and industries in Bangladesh. About one-eighth of the population relies on solar energy. For rural, off-grid communities, the availability of light during dark hours has extended productivity. Work can continue into the night and children have additional time to study. Solar energy has also increased the demand for electric-powered home utilities and replaced old agricultural methods with solar irrigation (Hutt, 2020).

Photovoltaic (PV) Cells

Solar cells, or photovoltaic (PV) cells, generate an electric current when exposed to sunlight. Multiple cells can be placed in a series to increase electrical output (Kalogirou, 2014). A PV cell contains layers of a crystallized semiconductor element, such as silicon. Pure semiconductor materials do not have an electric charge until it has been ‘doped’ with an impurity. Two doped semiconductor layers of opposite charges are stacked together inside the cell to generate an electric field (Largent & Wenham, 2003). The semiconductor layers within a PV cell produce an electric current through the following process:

  1. Sunlight makes contact with the PV cell.
  2. Light within a certain wavelength dislodges the electrons within the cell’s semiconductor material and generates current.
  3. The electric field between the positive and negative layers acts as a diode and forces the current to flow in one direction (Kalogirou, 2014; Aldous, 2020).

Advantages and Disadvantages of PV Cells

PV cells provide the following advantages in Bangladesh:

  • The availability of electricity in rural areas reduces urban migration and can minimize the urban-rural divide.
  • PV cells are adaptable to off-grid infrastructures and harsh climates.
  • PV cells provide a free source of renewable energy without any moving parts or pollutants (Kalogirou, 2014).

PV cells also have the following disadvantages:

  • Solar plants are expensive to build.
  • Solar plants require large spaces and are difficult to place in densely populated areas of Bangladesh (Islam, 2018).
  • Most PV cells absorb solar radiation within a very narrow spectrum. One-fourth of the absorbed energy is converted into electricity (George Washington University, 2017; Kalogirou, 2014).

The Future of Solar Energy in Bangladesh

As Bangladesh’s demand for energy rises, so does the demand for fossil fuels (Islam, 2018). Meeting the energy demand with renewable sources remains a challenge. However, the country has one of the most expansive solar energy programs in the world, and the efficiency of photovoltaic systems continues to improve (Hutt, 2020; George Washington University, 2017).
